# Research: Data for a sub-100M, externalized-knowledge, non-Transformer reasoner

Labels: [VERIFIED: url] means the figure comes from a fetched primary or near-primary source. [ESTIMATE] is my own arithmetic or inference. [HYPOTHESIS] is untested.

## 0. The target and why data is the main lever

- GPT-3.5 reference scores from GPT-4 Technical Report Table 2: MMLU 70.0% (5-shot), HellaSwag 85.5% (10-shot), HumanEval 48.1% (0-shot), GSM8K 57.1% (5-shot) [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08774].
- Where a dense 135M model sits after 2T tokens: SmolLM2-135M gets HellaSwag 42.1, MMLU-cloze 31.5, GSM8K (5-shot) 1.4 [VERIFIED: https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M]. On GSM8K that is about 40x below GPT-3.5. Scaling up "more web tokens into a small dense LM" will not close the gap. The data has to teach procedures, plus how to use a datastore and tools. It should not teach facts.
- Compute budget: Kaggle free tier is 2x T4 or 1x P100, about 30 GPU-hours per week, GPU sessions up to 12 h, and a TPU v5e-8 (sessions up to 9 h) [VERIFIED (third-party, not Kaggle docs): https://aimultiple.com/free-cloud-gpu]. I could not reach Kaggle's official quota page, so re-check it in your own account.
  - [ESTIMATE] 2x T4 gives about 65 TFLOPS FP16 each in theory, so about 40 TFLOPS sustained at 30% MFU. That is about 4.3e18 FLOPs per week, or about 2.2e20 FLOPs per year.
  - Chinchilla-style 6ND for N = 1e8 parameters: 2.2e20 FLOPs buys about 370B training tokens per year. Realistically you will get about 50–150B token-passes, and much less if long reasoning traces dominate.
  - **Conclusion: we cannot afford to "read the internet". Every training token must be high-yield.**

## 2. Evidence on data efficiency

1. **Textbook-quality data.** phi-1 (1.3B) used ~6B filtered-web + ~1B synthetic tokens for ~8 epochs (~50B tokens seen) and reached 50.6% HumanEval / 55.5% MBPP. **phi-1-small (350M) reached 45%** [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.11644]. That is a narrow domain, but it shows that with curated data, at 350M parameters, it is roughly at par with GPT-3.5's 48.1% on code.
2. **Small, simple data makes tiny models fluent.** Models under 10M parameters trained on TinyStories write coherent multi-paragraph English, while 125M models trained on web data barely do [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07759]. Data simplicity and consistency trade directly against parameter count.
3. **A verifier plus synthetic data beats the teacher.** TinyGSM shows 1.3B+1.3B models scoring 81.5% vs a 77.4% teacher [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09241]. Test-time selection with a verifier adds 13.3 points over the generator alone. **This is the most important result for us:** spend parameters on verification and search, not memorization.
4. **Retrieval substitutes for parameters.** RETRO with a 2T-token database matched GPT-3 on the Pile with **25x fewer parameters**, and kept improving as the database grew [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426v1]. Knowledge scales with the datastore, not with N.
5. **Classifier-filtered web data beats raw web.** DCLM (fastText) and FineWeb-Edu (Llama-labeled edu classifier) are the current standard [VERIFIED: URLs above]. A caveat: one study found FineMath-4+ gave only marginal mid-training gains and odd RL behavior [VERIFIED (secondary summary): https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/finemath].
6. **Open-model distillation at scale works.** Magpie data (from Llama-3) used for SFT roughly matched an official instruct model trained on ~10M points [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08464v1].

## 3. Licensing and ToS: a hard filter

- OpenAI's Terms of Use prohibit "Use Output to develop models that compete with OpenAI" [VERIFIED: https://openai.com/en-GB/policies/terms-of-use/]. A project whose explicit goal is to beat GPT-3.5 is the textbook case. **Exclude TinyGSM, MetaMathQA, UltraChat (original), TinyStories, KoAlpaca (if confirmed GPT-made), and any ShareGPT/Alpaca/OpenHermes-derived instruction data from weights training.** They may be used only as inspiration for re-generation with open models. Note that the DCLM classifier was trained on OpenHermes, so it is a filter, not training text; I judge this low risk but it should be flagged [ESTIMATE].
- Preferred generators: Apache-2.0 / MIT open-weight models (Mixtral → Cosmopedia; Qwen, DeepSeek and others: check each card) and Llama-3.x (license chain plus attribution; its exact output-use clause was not verified here).
- Non-commercial (NC) sets, such as NuminaMath-CoT and Tulu's No Robots: fine for research, but keep a manifest so a commercial fork can drop them.
- BY-SA (Wikipedia): keep it in the **datastore** rather than in weights, which is architecturally convenient anyway.
- Note: generating data locally with a teacher LLM is allowed at **training time** under our fair-play rules. Calling any >100M network at **inference** is cheating. The teacher's parameters do not count toward our 100M, but the report must state the teacher and its size.

## 4. Contamination: risks and tooling

- n-gram overlap is the standard approach (13-gram since GPT-3; lm-eval-harness implements it) but it misses paraphrases. A Llama-2-13B trained on rephrased test sets hit 85.9 MMLU undetected by n-gram checks [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.04850v1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04244].
- Smaller n and the longest-contaminated-substring match work better [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03923v1].
- Specific risks for this design:
  1. **The datastore itself.** A retrieval index over the web will contain benchmark questions and answers (MMLU, GSM8K and HumanEval are all posted online). For a retrieval system this is a **bigger** risk than for a dense LM, because the model can copy verbatim.
  2. Synthetic generators (Mixtral, Llama) may have memorized test sets and regurgitate them into "synthetic" problems.
  3. Procedural generators (re-ARC) must stay on the training split.
- Required protocol [ESTIMATE / design]:
  - (a) 8- to 13-gram plus MinHash near-dup scrub of **both training data and datastore** against every eval set.
  - (b) Embedding-similarity scrub at cosine > 0.9 with your own retriever to catch paraphrases.
  - (c) URL blocklist of benchmark hosting sites.
  - (d) Retrieval-audit logging: for every eval answer, store the top-k retrieved passages and report the % containing the gold answer string.
  - (e) Run "post-cutoff" fresh evals (problems written after the datastore snapshot, e.g. new competition sets, LiveCodeBench-style) as the headline number.
- Existing tooling: lm-eval-harness decontamination [VERIFIED: https://huggingface.co/koichi12/llm-scripts/blob/main/scripts/yans/lm-evaluation-harness/docs/decontamination.md], the Tulu 3 decontamination tools [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15124], and the OpenMathInstruct-2 contamination explorer.

## 5. Bold data strategy recommendation: "Procedures in weights, facts in the store, truth from verifiers"

The core thesis [HYPOTHESIS]: a model under 100M cannot memorize GPT-3.5's ~175B-parameter worth of facts. GPT-3.5's size is itself unconfirmed, so treat it as a [ESTIMATE]. It can, however, learn a compact **library of procedures**: how to decompose, query, read, compute, verify and write. The data should therefore be split by *what it teaches*, and the token budget should go overwhelmingly to procedures.

### Layer A — The External Knowledge Store (not trained into weights; scales without bound)
- Contents: FineWeb-Edu-dedup (220B tokens) + Wikipedia (multilingual, incl. kowiki) + FineWeb-2 Korean + OpenWebMath/arXiv + Python-Edu/Stack-v2 permissive + Cosmopedia, chunked to ~128–256 tokens.
- [ESTIMATE] Size: 300B tokens at 256 tokens per chunk gives ~1.2B chunks. With 64-dim int8 product-quantized embeddings (~16 bytes per vector with PQ) that is ~20 GB, plus BM25 over the same chunks. That fits a laptop SSD. You can start with 10–50B tokens.
- What scales: datastore tokens. RETRO-style evidence says quality keeps rising up to 2T tokens or more.

### Layer B — Procedure Curriculum (trained into weights; ~70% of the gradient budget)
1. **Verifiable procedural generators** (unlimited, free, clean): Reasoning Gym (100+ tasks), re-ARC (400 generators), plus our own generators for arithmetic and algebra (SymPy-checked), logic and SAT, graph algorithms, unit conversions, and code-execution traces (program → Python interpreter → exact output).
   - Difficulty is adaptive: keep each task at ~50–70% solve rate (an [ESTIMATE] target).
   - Contamination is near zero by construction.
2. **Tool-use traces**: the model emits `SEARCH(q)`, `READ(doc)`, `PY(code)`, `SOLVE(sympy)`, `CHECK(...)`, and the actual tool output is spliced back in. Train mostly on the *calls and the use of results*, not on the facts.
   - Source: KILT gives (question, Wikipedia provenance, answer) triples, so retrieval supervision is free.
3. **Open-model-distilled reasoning**: OpenMathInstruct-2 (CC-BY-4.0, Llama-405B), Magpie-style data regenerated from Apache-licensed teachers, and SmolTalk/Tulu (minus NC parts) for chat format.
   - Keep only samples that **pass an executable or symbolic check**. Rewrite every solution into the tool-call format, so the model learns "compute with Python", not "recall arithmetic".
4. **Korean procedures**: translate the procedural generators' templates, not the facts. Korean questions should retrieve from the Korean and English stores, and the model reads Korean as well as English (a cross-lingual retrieval curriculum).
   - The language "fluency" share stays small: ~5–10B tokens of FineWeb-Edu + FineWeb-2-ko + Cosmopedia, curated TinyStories-style for simplicity at first [ESTIMATE].

### Layer C — Self-play flywheel (what makes it scale past 0.1B)
1. Train the model as **generator + verifier** (TinyGSM shows the verifier is worth more than 13 points).
2. Sample N solutions per problem with tools, and keep the ones the verifiers or executors accept. These become the next round's training data (STaR/expert-iteration style).
3. A **problem-proposer** head writes new problems near the frontier of its own solve rate. A solution is only accepted with an executable check.
4. The data generator is now the model plus tools, so the data supply grows with compute, not with dollars.

### Token budget for v0 (one Kaggle-year, ~50–150B token-passes) [ESTIMATE]
| Bucket | Share |
|---|---|
| Language fluency (curated edu, en+ko) | 15% |
| Retrieval-reading (KILT-style + synthetic QA over the store) | 20% |
| Procedural verifiable curriculum | 35% |
| Open-model distilled + tool-rewritten math/code/chat | 20% |
| Self-play generated (grows over time) | 10% → 50% |

### What scales, and how
- **Params (0.1B → 1B → 10B):** a larger procedure library and longer working memory. The data mix stays the same and the curriculum difficulty rises.
- **Datastore (50B → 2T+ tokens):** facts and coverage. No retraining is needed beyond the index (RETRO evidence).
- **Test-time compute:** number of samples × verifier × search depth (TinyGSM evidence).
- **Self-generated verified data:** grows with compute; it is the only bucket with no human ceiling.

### Honest walls
- **Open-ended chat and instruction following** are the hardest domain for us. GPT-3.5's chat quality is not verifiable, and it comes from RLHF on massive human preference data. [HYPOTHESIS] Retrieval of good human-written answers plus a small generator gets partway there. Expect this to be the last domain we win, if we win it at all.
- **Fluency per parameter**: below ~100M, generation quality is bounded. TinyStories shows it is achievable only in a narrow register. We need a strong tool/retrieval scaffold so the model writes less from scratch.
- **Datastore contamination** can make a "win" fake. The audit protocol in section 4 is mandatory before any claim.
- **Biosafety**: when the north-star bio corpora (PubMed, UniProt, ChEMBL) are added, exclude dual-use pathogen and toxin enhancement content from both store and training. Gate any wet-lab-actionable generation behind human review.
